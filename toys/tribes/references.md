# Tribes of Behavior — references

The literature behind the toy and its database (ADR-0003, `docs/adr/0003-tribes-atlas-database.md`), kept with the project so it can be posted with it. `references.bib` holds the same entries for Zotero or BibTeX.

**Status of each entry:** ✅ means the claim attributed to the source was checked against the source itself on 2026-10-25. ◐ means the bibliographic details were checked but the claim rests on the work's standard reading or on secondary accounts. Links only, no copies: several of these are under copyright.

The toy's *historical* sources (the histories of Iberia behind each keyframe) are not listed yet. Today's data is cited as an authored schematic sketch. When the atlas is built, those sources live as `Source` rows and export to `data/sources.json`.

## Method: how the atlas stores claims, disagreement and uncertainty

| | reference | used for |
|---|---|---|
| ✅ | **Pelagios Network. *PLATO — Place Attestation Ontology*** (supersedes Linked Places Format), and the **World Historical Gazetteer v4 glossary**. <https://pelagios.org/place-attestation-ontology/index-en.html> · <https://docs.whgazetteer.org/content/v4/user-guide/reference/glossary.html> | The model is the *attestation*, "a source-backed claim that connects a Thing to information". That is ADR-0003's rule that every assertion row carries a citation. The confidence words (*certain, probable, uncertain, speculative*) come from WHG, which pairs them with an optional 0.0–1.0 number. The spatial precision (*exact, approximate, uncertain*) and temporal precision (*year, decade, century, era…*) fields also come from WHG. |
| ✅ | **Turchin, P., Brennan, R., Currie, T. E., Feeney, K., Francois, P., Hoyer, D., Manning, J., Marciniak, A., Mullins, D., Palmisano, A., Peregrine, P., Turner, E. A. L., & Whitehouse, H. (2015). Seshat: The Global History Databank. *Cliodynamics* 6, 77–107.** <https://doi.org/10.21237/C7clio6127917> · method page <https://seshatdatabank.info/methods> | Precedent for a global history database built for statistical analysis. Disagreements "in the literature or among Seshat experts" are recorded side by side, and estimated magnitudes carry "the likely range of variation". These justify keeping accounts side by side and storing an optional numeric range only when a source gives one. |
| ◐ | **Slingerland, E., et al. (2018) critique and Turchin et al. (2018) reply, *Journal of Cognitive Historiography* 5.** | A warning from the same project: data quality depends on who codes, and on "data filling". This is why ADR-0003 records `recorded_by` separately from the citation. |
| ✅ | **Mastrandrea, M. D., Field, C. B., Stocker, T. F., Edenhofer, O., Ebi, K. L., Frame, D. J., Held, H., Kriegler, E., Mach, K. J., Matschoss, P. R., Plattner, G.-K., Yohe, G. W., & Zwiers, F. W. (2010). *Guidance Note for Lead Authors of the IPCC Fifth Assessment Report on Consistent Treatment of Uncertainties.* IPCC.** <https://www.ipcc.ch/site/assets/uploads/2017/08/AR5_Uncertainty_Guidance_Note.pdf> · companion paper: Mastrandrea et al. (2011), *Climatic Change* 108(4), 675–691, <https://doi.org/10.1007/s10584-011-0178-6> | Two separate measures. **Confidence** is qualitative (*very low … very high*) and judged from evidence (*limited / medium / robust*) and agreement (*low / medium / high*). **Likelihood** is numeric and used only "when appropriate probabilistic information is available". The atlas copies the split: a word always, a number only when it is real. Agreement is not stored per citation; it is computed from how many independent citations concur. |
| ◐ | **Kent, S. (1964). Words of Estimative Probability. *Studies in Intelligence* 8(4).** <https://www.cia.gov/resources/csi/studies-in-intelligence/archives/vol-8-no-4/words-of-estimative-probability> | Readers translate the same estimative word into very different odds. So the mapping from confidence word to number is a stored, versioned function (`confidence_scale`), not a convention in someone's head. |
| ✅ | **Bleiholder, J., & Naumann, F. (2006). Conflict Handling Strategies in an Integrated Information System. WWW 2006 workshop paper, Edinburgh.** <https://hpi.de/oldsite/fileadmin/user_upload/fachgebiete/naumann/publications/PDFs/2006_roth_system.pdf> · survey: **Bleiholder, J., & Naumann, F. (2009). Data fusion. *ACM Computing Surveys* 41(1).** <https://doi.org/10.1145/1456650.1456651> | A classification of conflict strategies. The home-geo rule is **"Trust your friends"**: "whom to trust is decided once and carried out for all data values". The side-by-side view is **"Pass it on"**. "Strongest account wins" is a value-based deciding strategy. Naming them this way makes the reconciliation rule a known strategy, not an invention. |
| ◐ | **Openshaw, S. (1984). *The Modifiable Areal Unit Problem.* CATMOG 38. Norwich: Geo Books.** | Results depend on how the zones are drawn. The merged field changes where box edges fall, so ML features should also be built per account, not only on the reconciled map. |
| ◐ | **Tobler, W. R. (1970). A Computer Movie Simulating Urban Growth in the Detroit Region. *Economic Geography* 46 (supplement), 234–240.** | The first law of geography: near things are more related than distant things. This is the argument for a later `reconcile v2` that blends across geo borders instead of switching sharply at a seam. |
| ✅ | **Sandve, G. K., Nekrutenko, A., Taylor, J., & Hovig, E. (2013). Ten Simple Rules for Reproducible Computational Research. *PLOS Computational Biology* 9(10), e1003285.** <https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1003285> | Record how every result was produced, version every script, and record exact program versions. Every render, export and model run names its `FunctionVersion`s, `DataRelease` and code commit. |
| ◐ | **W3C (2013). *PROV-O: The PROV Ontology.* W3C Recommendation.** <https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/> | Shared vocabulary for "this run *used* these function versions and *was derived from* this release". It lets exports state provenance in a standard form. |
| ◐ | **MDN Web Docs. Content-Security-Policy: `script-src` — `'unsafe-eval'`.** <https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/HTTP/Headers/Content-Security-Policy/script-src> | Running JS that arrives as data needs `eval` or `new Function`, which a strict content security policy forbids by name. This is one reason the page implements function versions instead of executing them. |

## Theory: the Medium fiber and the channels

| | reference | used for |
|---|---|---|
| ◐ | **Carey, J. W. (1983). Technology and Ideology: The Case of the Telegraph. *Prospects* 8, 303–325.** Reprinted in *Communication as Culture* (1989). | The telegraph separated communication from transportation. This is the toy's "signals outrun bodies" and the `carried` flag on channel kinds: a property of the **conveyance**. |
| ◐ | **Innis, H. A. (1950). *Empire and Communications.* Oxford: Clarendon. — (1951). *The Bias of Communication.* Toronto: University of Toronto Press.** | Media are biased toward time (durable, heavy: stone, parchment) or space (light, portable: papyrus, paper). A candidate attribute for medium tribes, and a reason to keep medium and conveyance distinct but linked. |
| ◐ | **McLuhan, M. (1964). *Understanding Media: The Extensions of Man.* New York: McGraw-Hill.** | "The medium is the message": the medium is a behavior that shapes those who use it. That makes Medium a **fiber of tribes**, not just a property of roads. |
| ◐ | **Ong, W. J. (1982). *Orality and Literacy: The Technologizing of the Word.* London: Methuen.** | Orality, literacy and print are different ways of taking in ideas, each a behavior an observer could see. These are the first rows of the Medium fiber. |
| ◐ | **Eisenstein, E. L. (1979). *The Printing Press as an Agent of Change.* Cambridge: Cambridge University Press.** | The manuscript → print transition as a change in behavior, separate from any change in the roads that books travelled. |
